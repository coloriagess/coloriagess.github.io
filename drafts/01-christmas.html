Six Christmas coloring pages to print this weekend

Free PDFs, one sheet each, no account. One for the toddler, one for the kid who wants a challenge, and one for the adult who pretends not to color.

The first weekend of December is when the house changes. The tree goes up, the boxes come down from the attic, and somewhere in the middle of it there is a child who has been told to wait. A stack of coloring pages on the kitchen table is the oldest trick for that wait, and it still works. Here are six from the new Christmas coloring pages collection on Coloryla, chosen so that everyone at the table gets one they can finish.

For the littlest one

Start with the simple Christmas tree for toddlers: big baubles, one wide ribbon for a garland, a thick outline that forgives a crayon that wanders. A three-year-old finishes it in ten minutes and asks for another, which is the whole point.

The classic

Santa sliding down the chimney is the scene every picture book has and every child wants to color at least once. Boots first, sack held tight, bricks to fill in one by one for the kid who likes order.

Something that moves

No sleigh, no errand: a young reindeer playing in the snow, mid-leap, with snowflakes hanging in the air around it. It is the page that gets colored blue and white and then pinned to the fridge for a month.

For the patient one

The gingerbread house covered in candy is the busiest sheet of the set: icing drips, candy canes, gumdrops along the roof. It takes an hour with felt-tips. Hand it to the eight-year-old, or keep it for yourself.

Bedtime

An elf sleeping inside a Christmas stocking, chin on the edge, hat flopped over. Soft shapes, not much detail, the right page for the last twenty minutes before lights out.

For the one who thinks Christmas is corny

The Grinch stealing Christmas presents, sack stuffed past closing, grin included. Green, obviously. It is the page the ten-year-old picks while saying coloring is for babies.

How to print them

Each page has a print button and a PDF download. The PDF is laid out for A4 with a small margin and prints on one sheet. On US Letter, leave "fit to page" on and it lands fine. Print on the thickest paper your printer accepts, 120 g or more: felt-tips bleed through standard paper, and the fridge display deserves better.
